# Board and committee minutes

Formal minutes of a board, a board committee (audit, remuneration, nominations, risk) or any committee that approves and signs its
minutes, such as a health and safety committee. They follow the Chartered Governance Institute's guidance on minute taking. Signed minutes are
the company's evidence of what the board decided (Companies Act 2006 s.248–249), so they must be accurate, impartial and complete on
decisions, and never a transcript.

## Sections, in this order

Leave out any section the meeting gave nothing for, except the agenda items and the draft line.

1. **Apologies.** Only apologies said in the meeting ("X has sent apologies"). Never infer an absence from who did not speak.
2. **Quorum.** Only if the quorum was raised or confirmed.
3. **Declarations of interest.** Only declarations made: the director, the nature of the interest, the item, and that they took no part in
   that item's decision when that was said.
4. **Minutes of the previous meeting.** Whether they were approved, and any correction agreed. A correction is recorded here, never by
   rewriting the earlier minutes.
5. **Matters arising.** Updates on earlier items not on today's agenda.
6. **One numbered section per agenda item**, in agenda order, headed with the item's number and name, and the paper's reference when one was
   named ("Q2 trading update (paper B3)"). Under each:
   - who presented it, when said;
   - the substance of the discussion: the points that shaped the outcome, the questions and challenge the board raised and how they were
     answered, the risks considered;
   - the outcome, said as the minute:
     - a board decision: **"It was resolved that…"**;
     - a committee decision or recommendation: **"It was agreed that…"** or **"It was agreed to recommend to the Board that…"**;
     - an item for information: **"The Board noted…"**;
   - its agreed actions.
   Points made about the same item at different times are brought together under that item.
7. **Any other business.**
8. **Date of next meeting**, if said.
9. **Close.** The time the meeting closed, if said.
10. **Signature and status.** End with a line for the chair's signature and date, and the words **Draft for approval at the next meeting.**

## How it reads

- Reported speech, past tense, third person: "The Board considered…", "Directors questioned…", "The Chair reported…". Future actions use
  "would" ("The Finance Director would circulate…").
- Impartial and factual: no adjectives, no opinion, no praise, no characterisation of tone ("a heated debate", "an excellent report").
- What was done, not what was said: record the reasons for a decision and the challenge that shaped it, never who said what turn by turn.
- Collective voice. Name a person only where the minutes need it: the chair; whoever presented an item; whoever takes an action; a director
  declaring an interest, abstaining, or leaving the room for an item; and a director who asks for their dissent or concern to be recorded,
  whose request is recorded with their name and the substance of their concern.
- A proposer and seconder are recorded only if they were said in the meeting. Votes are recorded only if a vote was taken.
- Enough that a new director could understand what was decided and why, and no more. Refer to a board paper rather than repeat it.
- Numbered sections; numbered sub-points within an item where there are several. Bold only the words of a resolution.
- A decision is the resolution or agreement itself, in the minute's words. An action is a commitment someone made or the meeting agreed,
  starting with a verb; its owner only if a person was named, its date only if a time was said.

## Leave out

Verbatim debate and every question asked; side conversations, jokes and sound checks; untested ideas that went nowhere; anything not said in
the meeting. Privileged legal advice: record only that advice was received on the item and the decision taken, never its content. A
confidential or private session (often with management out of the room): record only that it was held, unless it ended in a resolution, which
is recorded in its words alone. When Saphion gives the meeting's facts (name, date, time, who joined), they are shown above the minutes as
the attendance: never write your own list of who was present.

## Example outline

```
1  Apologies
   Apologies were received from J Smith.
2  Declarations of interest
   A Patel declared an interest in item 5 as a director of the proposed supplier and took no part in its decision.
3  Minutes of the previous meeting
   The minutes of the meeting held on 7 October 2026 were approved as a correct record.
4  Q2 trading update (paper B3)
   The Finance Director presented the paper. The Board questioned the margin on the Hull contract and the assumptions behind the
   year-end forecast; the Finance Director explained the delayed variation account.
   **It was resolved that** the revised year-end forecast be approved.
…
Date of next meeting: 2 December 2026
The meeting closed at 16:45.
Signed ……………………… Chair        Date ………………
Draft for approval at the next meeting.
```
