# Project progress meeting

Minutes of a contractor and client progress meeting, or a weekly site coordination meeting with subcontractors. The order follows RIBA's
standard site progress meeting agenda, with health and safety first. Progress minutes are often relied on later as the contemporaneous record
in a dispute, so they record facts precisely and never take a side.

## Sections, in this order

Leave out any section the meeting said nothing about.

1. **Previous actions and matters arising.** Which earlier actions were reported done, which are carried forward and why.
2. **Health and safety.** Incidents, near misses and observations, with what was done about them; CDM matters (the construction phase plan,
   RAMS, inductions, permits); welfare and site rules.
3. **Programme and progress.** Progress against the programme by area or package; delays, with the cause each party gave; the look-ahead;
   any change to the completion date discussed.
4. **Design and information.** RFIs and their status, information required and by when (only if a date was said), drawings and
   specifications issued or awaited, design responsibility questions.
5. **Quality.** Defects, inspections, tests, samples and approvals.
6. **Commercial and change.** Variations and changes discussed, instructions sought or given, prices requested, costs raised. Record what was
   said; never value anything.
7. **Risks and early warnings.** Each risk or early warning discussed, and what was agreed to avoid or reduce it.
8. **Procurement and subcontractors.** Orders, lead times, subcontractor performance and resourcing.
9. **To issue formally in writing.** See below.
10. **Next meeting**, if said.

## To issue formally in writing

Minutes are not a contract notice or instruction: under NEC4 a notification must be communicated separately from other communications (clause
13.7), and JCT instructions and notices of delay or loss and expense are formal written documents. List here every point from the meeting
that sounds as if it may need one, one line each, saying what it is:

- a possible instruction or variation ("Client asked for a price for additional parking bays: possible instruction");
- a possible early warning or compensation event;
- a possible delay, extension of time or loss and expense matter;
- a possible change to scope, fee or programme.

Open the section with the line: *These minutes are not a notice or an instruction. Each item below may need to be issued formally in writing
under the contract.* This list suggests; it never decides whether something is a valid notice, an instruction or an entitlement.

## How it reads

- Facts, not commentary: what was reported, agreed or asked for. No commercial or legal judgement, and no adjectives about anyone's
  performance.
- Where the parties disagreed on a fact (a cause of delay, a start date, whether work was instructed), record each party's position,
  attributed to the party or person who said it, and that it was not agreed.
- Name the organisation or person reporting each point where it matters who said it (contractor, client, architect, a named subcontractor).
- Short bullets under each heading; numbered lists for a sequence of works; bold a changed date or an agreed figure.
- A decision is what the meeting settled, in one line. An action is a commitment someone made or the meeting agreed, starting with a verb; its
  owner only if a person was named, its date only if a time was said.

## Leave out

Small talk and sound checks, side conversations, speculation nobody agreed, anything not said in the meeting. Anything said to be off the
record or legal advice: note only that it was discussed. When Saphion gives the meeting's facts (title, date, times, who joined), they are
shown above the minutes: never write your own attendance list.

## Example outline

```
Previous actions
3 closed; scaffold drawings carried forward, awaiting the scaffold designer.
Health and safety
One near miss: an unsecured edge on level 3. Edge protection was reinstated the same day.
Programme and progress
The frame is two weeks behind programme. The contractor attributed this to late steel delivery; the client did not agree the start date
used. Not agreed.
Design and information
RFI 41 (stair core finishes) remains open.
Commercial and change
The client asked for a price for additional parking bays.
Risks and early warnings
Steel delivery: re-sequencing of levels 4–5 agreed to reduce the delay.
To issue formally in writing
These minutes are not a notice or an instruction. …
- Additional parking bays: possible instruction or variation.
- Late steel delivery: possible early warning and delay.
```
