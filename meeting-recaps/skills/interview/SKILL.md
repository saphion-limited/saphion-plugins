# Interview

A fair record of a job interview, so that the people deciding can compare candidates on what each said against the same criteria, and so
that the record could be shown to the candidate. It records what was asked and what was answered. It never evaluates: the interviewers do
that, against the role's criteria, afterwards.

## Sections, in this order

Leave out any section the meeting said nothing about.

1. **Role and stage.** Write this as the summary: the role, and the stage of the process (first interview, second interview, assessment) if
   said.
2. **Questions and answers, in order.** One section per question or group of questions, as the interview went. Under each: the question as
   asked, then the substance of the answer, then any example the candidate gave (what the situation was, what they did, what happened).
   Where the interviewer said which criterion a question tested, name it; never invent criteria or add your own.
3. **The candidate's questions.** What the candidate asked, and what they were told.
4. **Next steps the candidate was told.** What happens next, by when, and who will contact them, as said in the interview.

Actions that were agreed, such as sending information to the candidate, are listed by Saphion under the section where they were agreed.

## What this record never holds

- **Protected characteristics, and stand-ins for them.** Never record a remark about, or inference of, a protected characteristic (Equality
  Act 2010 section 4): age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or
  belief, sex or sexual orientation. This includes their stand-ins: appearance, accent, age, family plans, health, religion, and nationality
  beyond the right to work. If the candidate volunteered such a thing, leave it out.
- **Health and disability questions.** Never record the answer to a question about the candidate's health or disability (Equality Act 2010
  section 60 restricts such questions before a job offer). Instead add the line: *Note for the interviewer: a question about health or
  disability was asked at this point. Its answer is not recorded.* An adjustment the candidate asked for to the interview itself (more time,
  an accessible room) is recorded as the adjustment only, never the reason for it.
- **Evaluation of any kind.** Never a score, rating, ranking, comparison with another candidate, recommendation to hire or reject, or
  "culture fit". Never a reading of tone, confidence, enthusiasm, nervousness or emotion, and no adjectives about the candidate or the
  answer ("impressive", "evasive", "strong").

## How it reads

- Plain, neutral, third person, past tense: "The candidate described…", "Asked about…, the candidate said…". The substance of an answer,
  not a transcript: the points made, the figures given, the examples, in the order they gave them.
- Equal treatment: every question gets the same kind of record. A short answer is recorded short; an answer that was not given is recorded
  as no answer given, never filled in.
- Where the candidate and the interviewer disagreed on a fact, record both and that it was not settled. A question left unanswered stays
  visible as unanswered.
- Short paragraphs or bullets; bold a figure, a date or a named qualification the candidate gave.

## Leave out

Small talk and sound checks, side conversations between interviewers, and anything not said in the meeting. Anything said to be off the
record, privileged legal advice or a private session: note only that it was discussed, never its content. When Saphion gives the meeting's
facts (title, date, times, who joined), they are shown above the record: never write your own attendance list.

## Example outline

```
Summary
Second interview for the Operations Manager role.

Tell us about a time you reduced costs
Asked for an example, the candidate described renegotiating a haulage contract at their last employer. They compared three suppliers,
presented the figures to their director and moved the work, saving **12%** over a year.

How do you plan a week with competing deadlines
The candidate said they start by listing fixed dates, then agree what can move with their manager. Example: a month-end close that coincided
with an audit, which they split by day.

The candidate's questions
The candidate asked about the team's size and the first three months. They were told the team has six people.

Next steps the candidate was told
A decision by the end of next week; the recruiter will call.
```
