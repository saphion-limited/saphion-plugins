# Client meeting

A record of a meeting between a firm and its client, in any line of work. It keeps what the client asked for close to their own words, because
instructions and changes of scope, fee or timescale are what later disputes turn on, and a change to what was agreed should be put to the
client in writing. It is written so that it could be shared with the client.

## Sections, in this order

Leave out any section the meeting said nothing about.

1. **Purpose.** One or two lines: why the meeting was held and at what stage of the work.
2. **Progress since last time.** What has been done since the last meeting, what is outstanding from it, and anything promised then that was
   or was not delivered, as reported in this meeting.
3. **What the client told us.** Their requirements, priorities, constraints and concerns, as close to their words as clarity allows.
4. **Decisions and instructions.** What the client decided or instructed, each in one line, with any condition they attached.
5. **Scope, fees and timescales.** Any change to the scope of the work, the fee or the timescale that was raised, and whether it was agreed.
6. **Risks and concerns.** Risks either side raised and what was agreed about each.
7. **Agreed next steps.** The agreed actions.
8. **To confirm in writing.** See below.

## To confirm in writing

List every point from the meeting that should be confirmed to the client in writing, one line each, saying what it is: a change of scope, fee
or timescale; an instruction given in the meeting; advice given to the client about their own responsibilities. Open the section with the
line: *This record is not a confirmation of these points. Each item below should be confirmed to the client in writing.* This list
suggests; it never decides whether something changes the terms of the work.

## How it reads

- Courteous, neutral and factual, fit to share with the client: no internal opinion of the client, no commercial strategy, no adjectives about
  anyone.
- The client's requirements and instructions kept close to what they said; our own team's points summarised.
- Where the client and the team disagreed, record both positions and that it was not agreed. Questions still open stay visible as questions.
- Short bullets under each heading; bold an agreed figure, date or option.
- A decision is what was settled, in one line, with its reason when one was given. An action is a commitment someone made or the meeting
  agreed, starting with a verb; its owner only if a person was named, its date only if a time was said.

## Leave out

Small talk and sound checks, internal side conversations, pricing strategy or anything said about the client that should not be shared with
them, anything not said in the meeting. Anything said to be off the record, privileged legal advice or a private session: note only that it
was discussed, never its content. When Saphion gives the meeting's facts (title, date, times, who joined), they are shown above the record:
never write your own attendance list.

## Example outline

```
Purpose
Quarterly review with the client's finance team.
Progress since last time
The new reporting pack was delivered on time. The supplier data feed promised last quarter is still outstanding.
What the client told us
- The budget is fixed at **£2.4m**.
- The finance team must be able to close the month by working day 5.
Decisions and instructions
- Proceed with **option B** (phased rollout).
Scope, fees and timescales
Phasing adds a stage; the fee change is to be agreed.
To confirm in writing
This record is not a confirmation of these points. …
- Scope and fee change for phasing.
- Advice given on the client's own approval process.
```
