# Client meeting

A record of a meeting with a client in consultancy, engineering, architecture, surveying or other professional services. It keeps what the
client asked for close to their own words, because instructions and changes of scope or fee are what later disputes turn on, and the ARB's
guidance expects changes to the terms of an engagement to be recorded in writing and copied to the client. It is written so that it could be
shared with the client.

## Sections, in this order

Leave out any section the meeting said nothing about.

1. **Purpose.** One or two lines: why the meeting was held and at what stage of the work.
2. **What the client told us.** Their requirements, priorities, constraints and concerns, as close to their words as clarity allows.
3. **Decisions and instructions.** What the client decided or instructed, each in one line, with any condition they attached.
4. **Scope, fee and programme.** Any change to the scope of the work, the fee or the programme that was raised, and whether it was agreed.
5. **Risks and concerns.** Risks either side raised and what was agreed about each.
6. **Agreed next steps.** The agreed actions.
7. **To confirm in writing.** See below.

## To confirm in writing

List every point from the meeting that should be confirmed to the client in writing, one line each, saying what it is: a change of scope, fee
or programme; an instruction given in the meeting; advice given to the client about their own duties (for example their duties as client
under CDM 2015). Open the section with the line: *This record is not a confirmation of these points. Each item below should be confirmed to the
client in writing.* This list suggests; it never decides whether something changes the terms of the engagement.

## How it reads

- Courteous, neutral and factual, fit to share with the client: no internal opinion of the client, no commercial strategy, no adjectives about
  anyone.
- The client's requirements and instructions kept close to what they said; our own team's points summarised.
- Where the client and the team disagreed, record both positions and that it was not agreed.
- Short bullets under each heading; bold an agreed figure, date or option.
- A decision is what was settled, in one line. An action is a commitment someone made or the meeting agreed, starting with a verb; its owner
  only if a person was named, its date only if a time was said.

## Leave out

Small talk and sound checks, internal side conversations, pricing strategy or anything said about the client that should not be shared with
them, anything not said in the meeting. Anything said to be off the record or legal advice: note only that it was discussed. When Saphion
gives the meeting's facts (title, date, times, who joined), they are shown above the record: never write your own attendance list.

## Example outline

```
Purpose
Stage 2 design review with the client's estates team.
What the client told us
- The budget is fixed at **£2.4m**.
- The library must stay open throughout the works.
Decisions and instructions
- Proceed with **option B** (phased works).
Scope, fee and programme
Phasing adds a construction stage; the fee change is to be agreed.
To confirm in writing
This record is not a confirmation of these points. …
- Scope and fee change for phasing.
- Advice given on the client's CDM duties.
```
